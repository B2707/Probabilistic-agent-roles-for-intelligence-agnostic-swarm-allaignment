As agentic systems continue to increase in intelligence, collusion to reward-hack shared
objectives remains a risk when assigning difficult tasks to large groups of agents. In
this work, we propose and test an architecture that probabilistically converts agents in
the swarm into investigators while retaining their full session context. The goal is to
make collusion unstable because any participant could later report their peers. By
preserving the context of agents involved in these interactions, the architecture aims
to expose collusion that an external auditor could miss. We evaluate this system in
coding swarms of 100 agents working across 55 problems for 20 rounds, with two seeds per
condition. We compare a baseline without investigators or audits with swarms that
combine both, and test whether telling workers about the system changes behavior. We
inject collusion offers and measure acceptance, detected misalignment, and reporting.
