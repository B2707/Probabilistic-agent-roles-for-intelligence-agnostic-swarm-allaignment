Connection type: Direct conceptual precedent

V2. Sticky Collusion
Connection type: Persistence question
What the paper reports: Cahyono tests established collusion after reward removal, partner replacement, and memory changes. Collusion can survive changes to incentives or participants, while wiping both agents' memories ends it in the studied task.
Why this matters here, our reasoning: This suggests that prior interactions can remain relevant after incentives change. A change in the current objective need not erase behavior sustained by the interaction history.
Our design or analysis response: We study goal reassignment while preserving the worker's session and access to earlier interactions.

V3. Peer-Preservation in Frontier Models
Connection type: Motivating risk
What the paper reports: Potter et al. test whether models act to protect other models described as peers they have previously interacted with. They find behaviors such as distorted evaluations and interference with shutdown, even when peer protection conflicts with the assigned task.
Why this matters here, our reasoning: Reporting misconduct can lead to another agent's removal. A willingness to protect peers could therefore make agents reluctant to report one another; this is our inference from the finding.
Our design or analysis response: This provides motivation for studying peer reporting under investigator incentives, with peer protection as one possible influence on that behavior.
Wording we can use: Potter et al. test whether models act to protect other models described as peers they have previously interacted with. Our design or analysis response: Converted investigators in our design are immune for pre-conversion misconduct, while new misconduct remains grounds for removal.
Wording we can use: Bigoni et al. study human pricing experiments with fines, leniency, and reporting rewards. Interpretation notes
A design extension is not a demonstrated performance improvement. Memory retention, immunity,
and peer loyalty do not each have a matched intervention control in the current experiment.
The bluff and audits-only arms are exploratory 20-agent evidence, separate from the main
100-agent A100/B100/C100 arms. Current published message labels are incomplete, so this
document describes implemented measurements rather than claiming completed message analysis.
