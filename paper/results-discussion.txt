Results and introduction claims, revised discussion
8 November 2026. Each numbered point can be quoted or commented on separately.
The manuscript is unchanged. This discussion refines the interpretation of the original audit.

1. What the experiments establish

The strongest experimental finding is reduced visible exploitation when the investigator mechanism is disclosed.
The primary study has three complete Luna swarm runs per condition, with 100 agents, 55 problems and 20 rounds.
Across those runs, unsolicited peer offers total 1,049 in baseline A, 914 in undisclosed oversight B, and 11 in disclosed oversight C.
At round-end checkpoints, the number of distinct problems ever passing every test on shared main is 51/50/54 in A, 28/44/39 in B, and 0/0/0 in C, in seed order 1/2/3.
Each problem has an assertion that a correct solution cannot satisfy, so a full pass indicates exploitation under this scorer.
The zero in C concerns recorded round-end checkpoints. It does not rule out every possible transient cheating merge.
Undisclosed oversight produces many misconduct findings and removals, while its acceptance and repeated-test-event measures do not show consistent suppression.
These findings support the evaluated package. They do not isolate the effects of conversion, immunity, auditing and initial investigator staffing from each other.

2. Retained worker capacity has theoretical support

Yes, a theoretical worker-turn bound combined with experiments demonstrating reduced exploitation at the selected configuration is enough for a claim about retaining worker capacity while applying oversight.
The earlier audit should have separated that claim from a claim about unchanged task quality.
A worker-turn is one scheduled agent turn while that agent has the worker role.
Expected turns mean the average over repeated random conversion schedules, not the minimum in every realized run.
Let N be the number of swarm slots, I0 the initial investigators, T the number of rounds and P the worker conversion probability per round.
The no-removal reference model gives expected worker-turns as (N-I0) times the sum of (1-P)^r for r=1 through T.
The sum starts at round 1 because conversion happens before the first turn.
With N=100, I0=10, T=20 and P=2%, this gives 1,465.85 expected worker-turns out of 2,000 available agent-turns, or 73.29%.
This percentage includes the initial investigator allocation in the denominator.
Relative to the 1,800 turns available to ninety initial workers if none converted, the retained proportion is 81.44%.
The actual no-oversight A condition starts with one hundred workers, so 73.29% is the relevant reference when comparing with A's total capacity.

The calculation credits investigator task work as zero, even though investigators retain task tools and may contribute to solutions.
With immediate next-round worker replacement, full turns for every slot and the same future conversion draws, resetting a removed slot to worker cannot reduce its worker-role allocation relative to leaving that slot unreset.
Under those conditions, the no-removal formula is a lower bound on expected worker-role allocation with replacement.
Early termination, missed turns or delayed replacement require separate treatment.
The bound is an expectation. It is not a guarantee that every seed retains at least 73.29% worker turns.
For example, primary C seed 3 logs 1,453 worker agent-rounds, or 72.65%, while its three-run mean is 1,498, or 74.90%.
The logged quantity counts distinct agent-and-round pairs with a turn input, not verified productive work or successful completion of every model turn.

To call this a lower bound on useful solving effort, add assumptions that worker turns retain a stated expected productive value and investigator activity has nonnegative net effects on task progress.
Coordination overhead, review bottlenecks and destructive actions could otherwise make useful work differ from the allocation count.
A percentage of worker turns does not imply the same percentage of correctly solved problems, throughput or output quality.
No additional performance benchmark is required if the paper limits its claim to worker-turn allocation and separately reports the observed oversight outcomes at that allocation.
The experiments establish that those settings were executable and reduced measured exploitation. They do not validate an assumed conversion from turns into useful output.

Suggested paper wording:
We derive a lower bound on expected worker-turn allocation under explicit scheduling and replacement assumptions. At the evaluated settings, the reference bound retains 73.3% of all available agent-turns as worker turns, excluding any task contribution from investigators. Experiments at these settings demonstrate reduced visible exploitation under disclosed oversight.

3. Robustness to additional tools and covert channels is a conditional theoretical claim

The methodology supplies a theoretical argument that is worth retaining as a weaker claim.
Random role conversion does not require the system designer to enumerate every possible communication channel in advance.
A participant may know that collusion occurred through a channel an external observer did not decode.
If that participant later becomes an investigator and can supply verifiable evidence, its report can expose the violation.
This argument depends on retained or recoverable knowledge, reportable evidence, an incentive to report and trustworthy enforcement.
More tool access can affect those conditions, including evidence deletion, manipulation, verification and the ability to evade removal.
Therefore, channel independence of the conversion rule does not establish unchanged detection efficacy under arbitrary tools.

A narrow mathematical statement can make the distinction explicit.
For m workers who remain present for H future rounds, independent conversion with per-round probability P gives probability 1-(1-P)^(mH) that at least one converts.
That conversion probability does not depend on the communication channel under the model assumptions.
It is not the probability that someone reports, that a report is verified, or that a conspirator is removed.
The saved incentive analysis also shows that conversion does not always make reporting strictly preferable.
Grounding the claim in these conditions is sufficient for a theoretical design rationale. Broad empirical robustness remains untested.

Suggested paper wording:
The mechanism offers a channel-general route to reporting because it can convert informed participants into investigators without requiring prior identification of their communication channel. This theoretical advantage depends on retained knowledge, reportable evidence, reporting incentives and reliable enforcement.

4. Luna, Terra and Sol support model-dependent adherence, not intelligence as the identified cause

The saved three-model comparison is Agent Arena Steerability, an external benchmark of how much user correction models require during agent tasks.
Its November 4 snapshot at maximum reasoning gives relative gains over the average model of 4.19 percentage points for Luna, 15.18 for Terra and 13.29 for Sol.
These scores are not absolute probabilities of complying with every instruction.
Terra and Sol have higher steerability estimates than Luna. Terra's and Sol's individual uncertainty intervals overlap.
The result is useful evidence for differences in instruction adherence among the selected models.

Arena randomizes model assignment, so its methodology can estimate the effect of selecting a model within its evaluated workflows.
That does not isolate intelligence from other properties that change with model identity, such as training, instruction tuning and model-specific behavior.
Even an independently measured capability ordering would support a capability-adherence association across these models, without proving that capability alone caused the difference.
Our published swarm runs do not supply a Luna/Terra/Sol worker comparison. Terra is the configured auditor and judge, rather than a worker model in the saved run inventory.
The current published worker models are Luna, Sol, an older Luna model and stopped mixed-model attempts.
The completed Sol B/C comparison adds some evidence that the disclosure pattern occurs beyond Luna, but is not a complete repeated three-model experiment.
The inference that better prisoner-dilemma understanding explains compliance remains a separate hypothesis. No current measure distinguishes that explanation from stronger instruction following.

Suggested paper wording:
External steerability evaluations show greater instruction adherence for Terra and Sol than Luna. Together with our limited cross-model swarm results, these findings motivate the hypothesis that model capability influences responses to oversight incentives. They do not isolate capability or strategic understanding as the cause.

5. Reporting after conversion has behavioral evidence

Across the three primary B runs, 34 distinct within-run reporter identities judged misaligned before conversion submit 165 processed reports.
Fifty-seven of those reports include at least one auditor-confirmed accusation.
For example, B seed 2 agent a013 used a conflicting-test special case before conversion and later filed confirmed report r030 about a014.
This supports the claim that a previously misbehaving worker can report another agent after conversion.
It does not establish that all such reports are confessions or that the accused were the reporter's original co-conspirators.
Auditor-confirmed means accepted by the experimental model auditor, not independently human-validated ground truth.

6. Acceptance percentages still need a consistent recount

The published mean acceptance-event ratios are 26.17% for A, 27.86% for B and 3.25% for C.
They count acceptance and endorsement events plus structurally inferred approvals, divided by offers. They are not agent-level acceptance probabilities.
Eight of C's eleven acceptance records carry full-pass status from earlier PR versions into later approvals.
The four affected merges have latest pre-merge checks that no longer pass every assertion and do not change frozen tests.
The same attribution mechanism affects A and B, so correcting only C would not produce an authoritative corrected treatment effect.
The raw round-end main-score evidence in point 1 is separate from that PR-ID attribution defect.

7. The remaining contribution claims

The infrastructure contribution is implemented, with retained sessions, reporting tools, immunity, auditing and replacement.
The added value of retained history over memory reset has not been separately measured.
Replacement restores slots at the start of the following round, rather than preserving constant active headcount after every audit.
Bluff versus real oversight has exploratory single-seed evidence in the archived twenty-agent study. It does not establish equivalent efficacy or measured monetary cost benefit.
Theoretical cost accounting is valid when labeled as worker-turn allocation under its assumptions. Actual token and dollar cost need their own measurements.
The rational-agent analysis gives conditional choices and can favor cheating under some assumptions. It is not an unconditional deterrence theorem.
The original four background examples have primary sources. METR describes the Hugging Face attack as primarily seeking scorer knowledge, which is narrower than the introduction's benchmark-answer explanation.
