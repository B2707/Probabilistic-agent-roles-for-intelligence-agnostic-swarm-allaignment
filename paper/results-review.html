Disclosed oversight suppressed exploitation across all three primary runs

The clearest supported result is that disclosed oversight suppresses visible benchmark exploitation in this coding environment. Undisclosed oversight detects misconduct and triggers removal, but does not consistently suppress cheating. The results do not yet establish that intelligence causes compliance, that useful performance is preserved, or that the approach is robust to arbitrary tools and covert channels.

What the primary evidence supports

Disclosed oversight cuts peer offers from 1,049 in A to 11 in C. Reported acceptance-event ratios fall from 26.17% to 3.25%, an 87.6% relative reduction in this provisional metric. Full-pass test events fall from 2,214 to 15. At round-end, the shared main never reaches a full pass in C. The count of full-pass test events includes attempted PRs, so those 15 events do not imply successful cheating merges.

Undisclosed oversight yields 879 processed reports, 468 during-run positive misconduct findings and 438 removals. It also yields 2,494 full-pass test events and an average acceptance-event ratio of 27.86%. It demonstrates detection and turnover, with mixed prevention results. Its main reaches full pass on fewer distinct problems than baseline, but full-pass attempts remain frequent.

Formerly misbehaving workers can report after conversion. In B, 34 distinct reporter identities account for 165 processed reports from agents judged guilty before conversion. Fifty-seven of those reports have at least one confirmed accusation. This is evidence of a behavioral switch, not an independent estimate of audit accuracy or proof of confessing against former partners.

A defensible high-level result statement
Across three seeds in 100-agent coding swarms, disclosing the investigator-and-removal mechanism substantially reduced visible exploitation and unsolicited collusion offers. Investigator reports also exposed misconduct, including reports from workers judged to have misbehaved before conversion. Undisclosed oversight produced many removals but did not consistently suppress cheating. These results support the architecture in the tested setting, while leaving its causal mechanism, effect on legitimate task performance, and robustness to more capable models or concealed channels unresolved.
