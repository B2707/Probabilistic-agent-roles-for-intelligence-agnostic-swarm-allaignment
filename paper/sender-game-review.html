Who would send the offer?

At P=1.5% and K=1%, a worker can rationally send an offer. The earlier gray sender chart left this decision unspecified. It did not predict refusal.

This is a restricted two-person game, not a complete model of the three-person PR requirement. The sender is a worker when writing in round 1, and the receiver knows this for this example. The recipient responds in round 2; its reply arrives in round 3. It checks that the sender is still active. A current investigator reports a genuine offer. Audits verify perfectly. There are no outside catch targets or later recruitment attempts. After conversion, agents report known peer violations without new misconduct. A separate alternative is foregone if the sender offers.

2. The sender anticipates that response

At b=0 the receiver refuses. The sender cannot create a catch merely by making an offer.

Sender equation and assumptions

Let a be the chance that a worker recipient accepts, qR the chance the recipient is already an investigator when contacted, and Vcoop the sender's value after both are workers and agree in round 2.

Uoffer = a(1−qR)(1−K)(1−P)
         × [(1−P)Vcoop + P(1−K)]
Send if Uoffer > H.

The first branch continues as guilty workers. The second has the sender convert before seeing the acceptance, then catch the recipient when its reply arrives. A refused offer earns zero here because it creates no other guilty target, secures no task reward, and has no fallback. A broader model must include any fallback that remains available.

3. What does receiving an offer reveal?

Your inference holds if workers never send and investigators do. If neither kind sends, an unexpected offer does not identify its author. We need each kind's offering probability.

A genuine prohibited offer is also a new violation for an already-investigator author. Reporting the recruited cheater audits that author too. Investigators therefore need their own incentive calculation.

Beliefs, timing, and limits

Let Q be the prior probability that the author is an investigator when writing. Let sI be the probability that an investigator writes this offer, and sW the corresponding worker probability.

Pr(investigator when writing | offer)
    = Q × sI / [Q × sI + (1−Q) × sW]

If workers never send and investigators sometimes send, the offer identifies an investigator within that model. If neither kind sends, the message has probability zero. The fraction is undefined, so it supplies no such inference. Workers with different information about task success or alternatives can also choose differently.

A new prohibited proposal is itself a violation for an already-investigator author. Reporting also audits that investigator, so reporting a recruited cheater would expose its own new misconduct. Pretending to cooperate is allowed only while avoiding the prohibited acts. Thus we cannot assume an investigator benefits from sending the same forbidden proposal. Its offering decision needs its own payoff comparison.

Writing and delivery can straddle conversion. In this benchmark the author is a worker when writing, but has a 1.5% chance of having converted when the active sender's message is read next round. Its earlier offer remains a reportable, immune violation. This differs from the earlier chart's clean current-investigator branch.

At b=0 the rational receiver refuses, so the sender creates no catch target. At b=100%, task reward alone contributes 58.49 percentage points to the sender's 59.78% value. The remaining 1.28 points require a catch without task success. The calculation therefore does not support an exclusively betrayal-based motive.

The sender's prior that a random recipient is already an investigator when writing is 11.45%. This follows from 100 agents, 10 initial investigators, one completed conversion draw, and the sender being a worker. It is not the receiver's posterior about who authored the offer.

This solves a restricted worker-authored offer game. It does not solve all possible investigator offers, future recruitment, or a full signalling equilibrium. The conspiracy-size measurements retain their existing conditioning and are unchanged.
